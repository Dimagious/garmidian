Garmy builds a dashboard from your own Garmin data, inside an Obsidian note.

You paste a Garmin Personal API key. Garmy loads up to a year of history into one note per day. A four-step wizard asks which sports and metrics you want, previews the dashboard on your own numbers, and writes it into a note as Dashy blocks. The note is plain markdown: edit it, copy a block to your home page, or uninstall Garmy and keep everything. From install to the first dashboard takes about a minute.

What is in the note

Dashy blocks: stats, dashy-chart, heatmap, progress and countdown, written as YAML you can read. Dashy draws them from properties in your daily notes. Garmy only writes the blocks and the day notes. Every dashboard names Garmin as the data source under its title.

Install.md

Install

Garmy is in Obsidian's community plugin catalog. It draws its blocks with Dashy, so install both.

  1. Settings›Community plugins›Browse
  2. Search for Garmy, then Install and Enable.
  3. Do the same for Dashy.
  4. Open the command palette (Ctrl/Cmd P) and run Garmy: Build a Garmin dashboard. The ribbon button does the same.

Garmy needs Obsidian {{minAppVersion}} or later. You also need a Garmin Connect account and a Personal API key, which a short guide walks through.

How it works.md

How it works: key, load, choose, preview

  1. Key. In Garmin Connect open Settings, then Personal API Key. Accept the terms, create a key and pick how long it lives. Garmin shows it once. Copy it and paste it into the wizard. Garmy checks it with Garmin before moving on.
  2. Load. Pick 30 days, 90 days or one year. Garmy reads steps, heart rate and stress, sleep, HRV and workouts, about 17 requests for a year, and saves one note per day in Garmy/data. Keep the window open until it finishes.
  3. Choose. Pick the groups (Recovery, Movement, Training) and the sports. Only what exists in your data is offered. Options hold an optional races folder and the language of the labels.
  4. Preview. The wizard draws the dashboard on your own numbers. Create a note or Insert into the current note.

What lands in your vault is one note per day with properties only. These are example values:

Garmy/data/2026-11-06.md
---
steps: 10866
resting_hr: 49
stress: 32
body_battery: 84
sleep_score: 79
sleep_duration: "7h 5min"
hrv: 61
run_km: 10.4
run_min: 51
---

Garmy writes a property only when Garmin returns the number: steps, resting_hr, stress, body_battery (the day's highest value), sleep_score, sleep_duration, hrv, and per sport run_km, run_min, bike_km, bike_min, swim_km, swim_min, strength_min. Folders are configurable in settings.

Kilometers or miles: choose in the wizard (More options) or in settings. In miles mode the distances are run_mi, bike_mi and swim_yd, because a pool is measured in yards. Switching converts the notes already on disk and asks Garmin for nothing.

Already keep Garmin numbers in your notes?

If Garmin Health Sync or your own script writes metrics such as steps or sleep_score into daily notes, the first step can find those folders and build the dashboard from them. No key, no loading. How that works.

Dashboards it builds.md

Dashboards it builds

Garmy writes only the blocks your data supports. A block whose data is missing is left out, so a dashboard built after three days is small and a rebuild a month later is bigger.

Eleven dashboards, chosen in the wizard

The third step is a library. A card whose data is missing is greyed out and says what it waits for, so the list follows your data: lifting has no distance, a swim page counts in yards in miles mode, and a sport you never do is not offered.

Recovery

Six cards with the latest sleep score, sleep duration, HRV, Body Battery, resting heart rate and stress. Under them the 7 day averages against your usual level, with a small trend. Then two charts: sleep with Body Battery, and HRV with resting heart rate. The sleep and HRV guide has the blocks to copy.

Movement

Steps per day as a 7 day average, a streak of days over 10,000 steps, a bar chart with a 10,000 goal line and a year map of daily steps shaded in bands from 7,000 to 14,000.

Training

Cards for run, bike, swim and strength over the last 7 days, each compared with the period before once you have 14 days of data. Weekly bars of minutes per sport, a month calendar with a dot per sport, a year map coloured by the sport you trained most that day, and monthly goals taken from your own last 28 days. The training log guide and the heatmap guide go through the blocks.

Races

Give the wizard a folder of notes that each carry a date property and the dashboard gets a countdown to the next race. Leave the folder empty and the part is skipped.

countdown
```countdown
columns: 2
items:
  - { label: Next race, field: date, source: Races, pick: next, icon: 🏁 }
```

Rebuild and sync.md

Rebuild and auto sync

Sync. Garmy syncs shortly after Obsidian starts and then every 6, 12 or 24 hours. You choose in settings, or turn it off. The default is every 6 hours, and Sync now sits next to it. A sync loads the days since the last one plus a margin of three, capped at 60. The three most recent days are rewritten on every sync, because a day is not finished until it is over. Older days only get missing properties filled.

Rebuild. A dashboard built after three days is small. A month later, run Garmy: Rebuild the dashboard in this note. Garmy regenerates its own part from the notes on disk, adds the charts, year maps and goals that were not possible before, picks up a sport you started since, and leaves everything you wrote around it alone.

Garmy's part of the note is between two comment markers. The choices it was built with are stored in one garmy property:

Garmy/My Garmin dashboard.md
---
garmy: "1|Garmy/data|recovery,activity,training||en||"
---

%% garmy:start %%

...the generated blocks...

%% garmy:end %%

The property lists the sports you switched off. A sport that shows up in your data later is included on the next rebuild.

Privacy and network use.md

Privacy and network use

  • The keyKept in Obsidian's secure storage. It is never written to a note, to data.json or to a log. Garmy exchanges it for a token that is valid for 24 hours and keeps the token in memory only.
  • NetworkRequests go to personal.connectapi.garmin.com and nowhere else, through Obsidian's requestUrl. First to exchange the key for a token, then to read your own data.
  • TelemetryNone. No analytics, no ads, no server of mine, no account.
  • Your dataStays in your vault. If the vault syncs (iCloud, git, Obsidian Sync), the day notes sync with it like any other note.
  • FilesGarmy creates notes in the data folder and the dashboard folder you configure, and changes only the dashboard part of a note you ask it to rebuild. It looks across the whole vault only when you click Look for those notes: it reads note properties from Obsidian's cache, opens no file and keeps nothing.
  • AttributionEvery dashboard names Garmin as the data source right under the title, as Garmin's API brand guidelines ask. Keep that line if you share a screenshot.
Read Garmin's terms

Garmin describes the Personal API as meant for individuals who want programmatic access to their own data, not for commercial applications serving multiple end users. Garmy runs on your computer with your own key. Read the terms you accept when you create a key.

Requirements.md

Requirements

  • Obsidian {{minAppVersion}} or later.
  • The Dashy plugin, which draws the blocks. Without it the note shows only the markup, and the wizard's preview warns you and can turn Dashy on or open its page.
  • A Garmin Connect account and a Personal API key you create yourself. Where to find it.

What it leaves out

  • Training Readiness and a VO2 max number. Garmin's Personal API does not provide them.
  • distance_km. The range endpoints report walking distance only, so Garmy writes no daily distance rather than show a wrong one.
  • Interpretation. Garmy draws your numbers and does not coach you.
  • Multi-sport activities are not split into their sports.
  • Phones. The plugin is not desktop-only and uses only Obsidian's own APIs, but nobody has run it on an iPhone or Android device yet.

If your notes already carry training_readiness or distance_km, for example from another plugin, Garmy's dashboard uses them in existing-notes mode.

Questions.md

Questions

Do I need Dashy?

Yes. Garmy writes Dashy blocks, and Dashy draws them. Install it from Community plugins and enable it. The wizard's preview tells you when Dashy is missing or turned off.

Where does my key go?

Into Obsidian's secure storage, and from there to Garmin's token service when Garmy needs a token. It is never written to a note or a file. Forget key in settings removes it.

The key stopped working. What now?

A key works for the lifetime you chose when you created it. When Garmin rejects it, Garmy says so once and settings show Replace key. Create a new key in Garmin Connect and paste it in the wizard.

Does it work on my phone?

Unknown. The plugin is not desktop-only, but it has not been tested on a phone yet, so treat mobile as unverified.

Will a rebuild overwrite my text?

No. Rebuild replaces only what is between %% garmy:start %% and %% garmy:end %%. Text above, below or around it stays.

Why is there no Training Readiness or VO2 max?

Garmin's Personal API does not provide them, so there is nothing for Garmy to read.

Can it overwrite the notes Health Sync wrote?

Only if you point Garmy's data folder at them. By default Garmy writes to Garmy/data. In a folder of your own notes, older days only get missing properties filled, and the three most recent days are overwritten with Garmin's latest numbers.

Is it free?

Yes, MIT licensed, and nothing is locked behind a payment. The plugin shows no ads and no donation prompts.

How do I report a bug?

Open an issue with your Garmy and Obsidian versions. Technical details of a failed sync go to the developer console, never the key.

If Garmy is useful to you

Garmy is free and made by one developer. A star on GitHub tells others the plugin is worth a look, and a coffee is a way to say thanks.
